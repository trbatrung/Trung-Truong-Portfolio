# Positioning & Revamp Blueprint

The strategic spine of the site revamp. Everything else (design, copy, structure) serves this.
_Decided with Trung, 2026-07-21._

## The core positioning
Move from **"video editor (who uses AI)"** → **"a producer who sees the picture through both
lenses — AI and real production."**

- Editing is ~90% of current revenue and the reliable front-door hire. It stays central.
- Consulting / advisory is the higher-ceiling upside.
- These are **one integrated offering**, not two tracks: *"I don't just advise on how to produce
  with AI — I hand you the final cut."* That fusion is the differentiator (most AI consultants
  can't execute; most editors can't advise).

## The golden rule (governs all copy & design)
**Show, don't tell. Let the visitor *conclude* "consultant" — never announce it.**

> Never state a level of value the page can't immediately prove on the same screen.
> Every big claim sits next to its evidence.

Declaring "AI Production Consultant" invites *"prove it"* and, with thin consulting proof, reads as
"talks big" — which actively costs the credible editing work (90% of income). So the site
**demonstrates** judgment and lets the visitor arrive at the conclusion themselves.

## The two-layer read
- **Skim layer (first 5 seconds):** plain, concrete, obviously a real editor/producer with strong
  work. An editing client instantly thinks *"this is my guy — hire him."* No buzzwords up top.
- **Depth layer (as they read on):** tool-vetting, decision→outcome framing, and the POV quietly
  upgrade the read from "editor" → "strategic partner" — without ever claiming the title.

Consulting is the **ceiling-raiser, not the gate.** Editing stays the low-friction front-door hire.

## Structural principle: proof before philosophy
Order the page so credibility is earned before opinion is offered:
**Hero → Work (proof) → How I think (POV) → What I do (engagements) → About → Contact.**
The manifesto never goes at the top — you earn the right to the opinion by showing the work first.

## Turning existing work into consulting proof (no case studies needed)
Consulting credibility is manufactured three ways from what already exists:
1. **Reframe every project by decision → outcome**, not by software.
   - ❌ "AI B-ROLL · VEO3 & KLING — Dermatology Course"
   - ✅ "Replaced a 2-day location shoot with AI-directed footage. Faster, cheaper, on-brand."
2. **"Tools I've vetted" R&D section** — judgment on display; no client required.
3. **The POV section** — demonstrated thinking *is* the audition for consulting.

## Engagement framing ("What I do")
Present as a ladder from advisory → execution (not a tool list):
1. **AI production strategy & tool selection** — figure out what the video is *for*, then pick the
   right tool for that use case (and flag when AI is the wrong call).
2. **Full-pipeline production** — script to final cut.
3. **Editing & post** — the craft layer; the reliable hire.

## The POV copy (locked draft — placed LOW on the page, after proof)

> ### Slop or salvation. Both are wrong.
>
> People treat AI video like it's either garbage or magic. Lean too hard either way and you ship
> something bad.
>
> The market doesn't help. Thousands of AI tools, all charging premium fees, each marketed like a
> specialist — Tool A "best for UGC," Tool B "best for long-form." Strip the wrapper and most do
> the same thing. Picking the right one for *your* use case — and knowing where it breaks *before*
> you've burned a month and a budget finding out — is most of the job.
>
> So I don't start with a tool. I start with a question: **what is this video actually for?**
> Pushing a product with UGC is a different problem than scaling short-form, and each has a
> different right answer. Give me the use case and I'll test five tools that claim to do the same
> thing and tell you exactly where each one falls apart.
>
> I'll also tell you when *not* to use AI. It's built for scale — not the one hero film that has to
> be perfect. It won't run your whole pipeline, and for most companies it doesn't need to. Knowing
> that line is the value.
>
> The rest is taste. I can usually tell what's wrong with a video in the first few seconds — and I
> know that past a point, fixing it makes it worse. AI gets you to 80% fast. Knowing that 80% is
> the finish line, and not chasing a perfection that quietly ruins the work, is the difference
> between shipping and spinning.
>
> Most producers see the picture through one lens — AI *or* real production. I see both. That's the
> whole job.

## Phase 2 (later, not now)
Individual **case-study sub-pages** for 2–3 flagship projects (problem → tools tested → decision →
outcome). This is the real payoff of multi-page and the strongest anti-grunt move — but only after
the single page is solid. **No editing-vs-consulting page split, ever** — it re-separates the two
crafts the whole positioning fuses.
