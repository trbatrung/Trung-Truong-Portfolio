# Tone & Voice

## Voice in one line
First person, confident, punchy. Short declarative fragments for emphasis. Senior and selective —
not eager. Sells outcomes, not features.

## The central narrative
The **"two crafts"** positioning: most producers know *either* AI tools *or* filmmaking — Trung
does both. That combination = premium-looking output, delivered faster and cheaper than a
traditional workflow. Every piece of copy should ladder back to this.

## Do
- Lead with the **outcome / value**, not the process ("looks like it cost three times as much").
- Name **concrete tools** (Veo3, HeyGen, ElevenLabs, Kling, Final Cut Pro) — specificity = credibility.
- Keep a **senior, selective** register ("select projects and senior roles").
- Use short, confident fragments to punch key claims.

## Don't
- Don't overclaim AI as a replacement for craft — frame it as **"an extension of it, not a replacement."**
- Don't get vague or buzzword-heavy; the credibility comes from specifics.
- Don't sound available-for-anything; the tone is curated, not desperate.

## Style conventions (observed on site)
- Dash style: spaced hyphen `" - "` (not em dashes).
- Section labels: ordinals `Nº1 / Nº2 / Nº3`.
- Heavy use of **uppercase micro-labels** for eyebrows/tags.

## Phrase bank (approved, reuse-friendly)
- "Two crafts. One producer."
- "Most producers either know AI tools or know filmmaking. I do both."
- "looks like it cost three times as much"
- "No shoot. No location scout. No stock footage compromises."
- "Not a replacement for craft - an extension of it."
